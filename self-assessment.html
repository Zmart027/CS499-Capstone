My Professional Journey

My journey through the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU) has been one of continuous learning, persistence, and discovering where my interests fit within the technology industry. When I first enrolled, I knew very little about computer science and was not entirely sure whether it was the right career path for me. My curiosity initially came from my fiancé, who works in the field, and my interest in understanding what made his profession so engaging. At the time, I was also pursuing a certificate in data analytics and considering how those skills might be applied in different industries, including healthcare. As I progressed through the program, my understanding of computer science expanded beyond programming languages and individual assignments. I became interested in how applications are structured, how their components communicate, and how development decisions affect the final product. This interest became more focused during CS-465, where working with a full-stack application helped me recognize the career direction I wanted to pursue.

My ePortfolio and Capstone Enhancements

This ePortfolio represents the technical knowledge and practical experience I developed throughout the program, with particular focus on software design and engineering, algorithms, and data structures, and databases. For my CS-499 Capstone, I selected Travlr Getaways, a travel application originally developed in CS-465 using the MEAN stack (MongoDB, Express.js, Angular, Node.js). I chose this artifact because it demonstrates how the frontend, backend, API endpoints, and database work together within a single application. Rather than developing an entirely new project, I revisited the original code to identify areas that could be improved.

My first enhancement focused on strengthening application security through JSON Web Token (JWT) authentication and role-based authorization. The second introduced trip searching and price sorting using Angular and TypeScript, while the third implemented database-side price filtering using MongoDB queries. Together, these enhancements demonstrate my ability to evaluate existing code, implement functionality across different application layers, and troubleshoot issues that arise when components interact.

Technical Growth and Software Development

Throughout my time and coursework at SNHU, I have gained a better understanding of software development practices that extend beyond writing functional code. One concept I particularly enjoyed learning was object-oriented programming (OOP), because it showed me how classes, objects, encapsulation, and inheritance can help me organize code into reusable and maintainable components. I also developed a greater appreciation for the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), including requirements analysis, design, implementation, testing, and maintenance.

Before entering the program, I viewed programming primarily as writing instructions that a computer could execute. I now recognize that developing software requires planning, evaluating design choices, and considering how changes may affect the application over time. This shift in perspective has influenced how I approach technical problems and has encouraged me to think about maintainability rather than focusing only on whether the code works.

Professional Communication and Growth

My academic experience also helped me strengthen my professional communication skills. Although most coursework was completed individually, discussion assignments provided opportunities to exchange perspectives and respond to classmates' ideas. Occasionally, when someone responded to my original discussion post, I continued the conversation rather than allowing it to end after the required responses. These interactions helped me become more comfortable explaining my reasoning and considering viewpoints different from my own.

Outside of technical knowledge, one of the most significant challenges I faced was balancing school with full-time employment and my child's school and extracurricular activities. Managing these responsibilities required consistency, organization, and the ability to prioritize competing commitments. Completing this degree is a major accomplishment in itself, but I also believe that each completed module and course deserves recognition as a smaller victory that contributed to reaching this point.

CS-499 Course Outcomes
Course Outcome 1: Collaboration and Organizational Decision-Making

The Computer Science program helped me understand how collaboration, technical communication, and code review contribute to informed decision-making in software development. Although I did not participate in formal group development projects, weekly discussions allowed me to exchange ideas with classmates and practice communicating technical concepts.

During CS-499, I applied a structured review process to the original Travlr Getaways application before implementing my enhancements. I examined the existing authentication and authorization logic, identified limitations in error handling and application functionality, and documented potential improvements. Conducting the review before modifying the code allowed me to identify and prioritize potential improvements while considering how each enhancement could affect the existing application.

Course Outcome 2: Professional Communication

Throughout the program, I became confident in my ability to communicate information clearly and professionally. This growth is reflected in my written coursework, technical explanations, and CS-499 deliverables.

For my capstone, I prepared a recorded code review explaining the structure of the original Travlr Getaways application, identifying weaknesses, and describing my proposed enhancements. I also developed three written enhancement narratives documenting the changes I implemented, the challenges I encountered, and the technical knowledge demonstrated through each improvement. My ePortfolio brings these materials together through a navigable website with project descriptions and source code links.

Course Outcome 3: Algorithms and Data Structures

My second enhancement demonstrates how algorithmic principles can improve application functionality and the user experience. I implemented trip searching and price sorting within the Angular administrative interface using TypeScript array operations.

The search functionality evaluates trip information based on user input, while the sorting feature uses numerical comparisons to organize trips by price in ascending or descending order. I maintained separate arrays for the original trip data and filtered results, allowing users to change search and sorting selections without permanently modifying the original collection. I selected a straightforward client-side implementation because it addressed the application's requirements without adding unnecessary complexity.

Course Outcome 4: Computing Techniques, Tools, and Industry Value

Working with Travlr Getaways strengthened my ability to use established development tools and techniques to implement practical software improvements. The MEAN stack required me to understand how Angular components communicate with backend services, how Express.js processes API requests, and how MongoDB stores and retrieves application data.

My third enhancement introduced minimum and maximum price filtering. I updated the Angular service to send price values through HTTP query parameters and modified the Express controller to construct MongoDB queries using the $gte and $lte comparison operators. During testing, I discovered inconsistencies betwteen the numeric price type defined in the Mongoose schema and some existing MongoDB records that stored prices as strings. Resolving this inconsistency reinforced the importance of consistency between application models and stored data.

Course Outcome 5: Security Mindset

My first enhancement allowed me to apply security principles by strengthening access controls within Travlr Getaways. Although the original application included JWT authentication, it did not adequately distinguish between regular users and administrators when protecting trip modification operations.

I introduced user roles and implemented authorization checks within the backend to restrict administrative functionality. I also updated the Angular interface so that regular users could view available trips without being presented with administrative controls. I learned that frontend restrictions alone do not provide sufficient security because API endpoints can be accessed independently of the interface. I used Postman, JWT.io, and MongoDB to examine authentication tokens, verify user information, and test access permissions for different account types.

Professional Goals and Conclusion

Completing the Computer Science program at Southern New Hampshire University has helped me develop clearer professional direction and a greater appreciation for the work involved in creating reliable software. My goal is to pursue a career in full-stack development, particularly because I enjoy designing interfaces that are intuitive and user-friendly while also understanding how the underlying systems function. I have always been interested in learning how things work and connect, and full-stack development allows me to explore that interest across multiple layers of an application.

Moving forward, I plan to continue developing my technical skills and expanding my knowledge of Agile practices, cybersecurity, data analytics, and computer hardware. I recognize that computer science is constantly evolving and the continuous learning will remain an important part of my career. Looking back, I am proud not only of completing the program but also of the smaller accomplishments, challenges, and lessons that brought me here. This ePortfolio represents the progress I have made from someone treading in unfamiliar territory to someone more confident and capable in the field of computer science.
